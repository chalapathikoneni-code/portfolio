Authority-free reconciliation

Selecting among conflicting sources using evidence and context rather than permanent source authority.

On this pageResearch questionCore ideaCurrent directionWhy it mattersWhere the work goes next

Research question

What should a system do when several sources disagree and none is consistently correct across all records, contexts, and times?

Core idea

Authority-free reconciliation treats source selection as a contextual decision. Evidence can include recency, completeness, agreement, domain constraints, historical behavior, and downstream consequences. The goal is not to declare one permanent winner.

Current direction

The work develops the idea of selection dominance: conditions under which choosing an existing source value is preferable to synthesizing or correcting a new one. It also examines trust as contextual rather than global.

Why it matters

Many enterprise systems use static precedence rules because they are simple to operate. Those rules become fragile when source quality changes or when authority differs by attribute, time, or business context.

Where the work goes next

The next step is a stronger theoretical characterization, broader baselines, careful ablations, and evidence that the problem appears naturally across multiple integration domains.
