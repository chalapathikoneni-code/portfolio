<header class="page-hero"><div class="wrap"><div class="eyebrow">Engineering philosophy</div><h1>The difficult part was never moving the data.</h1><p>It was preserving meaning while systems, teams, and business rules changed around it.</p></div></header>
<section><div class="wrap two-col"><div><div class="eyebrow">A change in perspective</div><h2>What experience changed.</h2></div><div class="prose"><p>Early in my career, I thought data engineering was mostly about getting information from one system to another. That view did not survive production.</p><p>The hardest incidents were not always broken jobs or missing files. They were cases where every technical check passed, yet two systems no longer agreed on what the business was doing.</p><p>That is why I now think of data engineering as the work of preserving meaning. Movement matters. Scale matters. Cost matters. But none of them is enough when the answer cannot be trusted.</p><blockquote>A successful pipeline is not the same thing as a correct decision.</blockquote></div></div></section>
<section><div class="wrap"><div class="eyebrow">Principles</div><h2>Ideas I return to.</h2><div class="record-grid"><article class="record"><h3>Systems fail quietly.</h3><p>The most expensive failures often look normal. Records arrive, schemas match, and jobs finish. The meaning is what drifted.</p></article><article class="record"><h3>Data quality is an engineering discipline.</h3><p>It belongs in architecture, control flow, ownership, and recovery—not only in a score shown after the fact.</p></article><article class="record"><h3>Reconciliation is a form of reasoning.</h3><p>When sources disagree, a system needs evidence, context, and explicit rules for choosing—not blind preference for one database.</p></article><article class="record"><h3>Trust should survive change.</h3><p>A dependable system remains understandable as business definitions, technologies, and organizational boundaries evolve.</p></article><article class="record"><h3>AI inherits the weaknesses of its evidence.</h3><p>Model quality cannot repair missing lineage, stale context, ambiguous semantics, or unreliable feedback loops.</p></article><article class="record"><h3>Recovery is part of correctness.</h3><p>It is not enough to detect an error. A system must show what changed, limit propagation, and support a safe return to service.</p></article></div></div></section>
