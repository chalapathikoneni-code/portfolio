<header class="page-hero topic-hero"><div class="wrap"><div class="eyebrow">Data Quality Engineering</div><h1>Building trustworthy data systems at scale.</h1><p>Data quality is not only about whether a record matches a schema. It is about whether the data still represents the business event, decision, or state that downstream systems believe it represents.</p></div></header><section><div class="wrap article-body"><aside class="article-aside"><strong>Chalapathi Koneni</strong><span>Senior Data Engineering Leader</span><a href="/engineering.html">Engineering work →</a><a href="/research.html">Research →</a></aside><article class="prose topic-prose"><h2>What dependable data quality requires</h2><p>My approach connects structural validation with semantic checks, reconciliation across independent sources, anomaly detection, lineage, observability, and evidence that can explain why a result should be trusted.</p><h2>Structural and semantic corruption</h2><p>Structural failures are visible when fields, types, or required records are missing. Semantic corruption is harder: the record is technically valid, yet its meaning has drifted, its source is stale, or it conflicts with another valid system.</p><h2>Engineering practices</h2><ul><li>Validate meaning as well as format.</li><li>Reconcile independent evidence instead of assuming a single source is authoritative.</li><li>Make data-quality decisions observable and explainable.</li><li>Design recovery paths, not only alerts.</li><li>Measure the quality of the evidence used during incident response.</li></ul><p>Related work: <a href="/research.html">public research</a>, <a href="/engineering.html">engineering case studies</a>, and <a href="/thinking.html">technical ideas</a>.</p></article></div></section>
