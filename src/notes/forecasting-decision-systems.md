<header class="page-hero"><div class="wrap"><div class="eyebrow">Engineering note</div><h1>Forecasting systems are really decision systems</h1><p>The model is only one component in the path from evidence to action.</p></div></header>
<section><div class="wrap article-layout"><aside class="toc"><a href="/writing.html">← All notes</a></aside><article class="prose">
Forecasting discussions often begin with model accuracy. Production systems force a wider view.

A forecast affects ordering, placement, pricing, labor, and inventory. Those decisions depend on product state, promotions, supply constraints, and timing. The model is important, but it sits inside a larger system of evidence and action.

That is why forecasting architecture should be judged by more than predictive performance. It should also preserve meaning, expose disagreement, and support recovery.
</article></div></section>
